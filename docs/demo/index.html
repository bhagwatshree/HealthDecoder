Why a separate table

Lab results and home readings never share a line

A glucometer reads capillary blood; a lab reads venous plasma. A home BP cuff and a clinic cuff don't even agree on what's "normal." So Trends has two tabs, not one merged chart — try switching Systolic/Diastolic to Home readings and log a new BP to see it land on its own line.

What's real, what's illustrated

Everything on-screen is the shipping build

Every screen, tile colour, and threshold here (135/85 for home BP, not the clinic's 140/90) is taken from the actual Android app. The camera scan itself can't run in a browser, so that one screen explains what happens instead of faking it.

On-device by default

Reports and home readings are stored encrypted on the phone. Nothing is uploaded except the page image sent once to an AI provider for extraction — identity details are blanked out first.

02

Built for families

One account can hold records for the people you actually manage — a parent, a child, yourself — each with their own trends, medicines, and reminders.

Ten languages, not just English

Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi, Odia — including the AI-generated medicine explanations, not just the menus.
